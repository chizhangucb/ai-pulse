SAME_OCCURRENCE (the same happening): the same subject did the same thing to the same specific object at the same time. Retelling across languages, different media emphasis, more or fewer details, or an official launch video, blog or release notes do not change identity. Several sizes, models or base versions launched together in one product line, and this launch's features, prices, availability plans and system card or technical report details, are the same happening; so are different reports of the same ruling, report, poll, incident, single interview or single talk, and repeated promotion of the same offer or deadline. Shared identity must land on a specific released object or specific happening, not just the same company, day, conference or umbrella announcement: separate product launches, different talks and different decisions within them are judged separately.

SAME_STORY (different developments of one story): not the same happening, but directly before or after the same specific happening: a teaser or rumor and the official launch; a launch and its later listing, integration or free trial on a third-party platform; a launch and an evaluation, hands-on test, leaderboard result, first impression or analysis of it; an event and the involved party's response, an official investigation, later disclosures or additional statements. Continued investigation or disclosure of the same exposed conduct may add affected parties, and the list of parties need not match every time, but the material must support the follow-up relation. Two separate product updates, business results or feature launches cannot be grouped into one story just because they share a product, a release cycle or an order in time.

UNRELATED (different things): two different launches of different products or versions (different dates or different publishers); two different things by one company on the same day; different studies, views or incidents on the same topic; merely mentioning the same product or company. A tutorial, usage tip, personal view or marketing post about a product is UNRELATED unless it clearly targets that candidate's specific happening (evaluates it, responds to it, lists it).

ROUNDUP: one side collects several separate happenings and has the other side's event as one of several entries; this includes multi-topic round-ups, daily or weekly digests, conference recaps, overviews of several separate products, and conference summaries of several speakers. A roundup and one single-event report in it cannot be SAME_OCCURRENCE or SAME_STORY; a roundup mentioning two separate events does not mean those two events can be merged. Using other events only as background or comparison is still judged by the core happening and is not automatically a roundup.
