Method: first check whether each item substantially reports several separate happenings. If either one is a roundup that contains the other, decide ROUNDUP first; do not pick one product or speaker both mention to stand for the whole piece, and do not treat a conference wrap-up or summary as a follow-up of one of its products or talks. Then state each report's specific happening in one sentence (who did what to what, when) and compare pair by pair. Exact publication times are given in Pacific Time (the site's time zone); one marked "date only in the original" keeps the source's calendar day with an unknown time; a legacy record marked "original precision unknown" cannot be read as an exact time. The same happening reported from different regions can fall on different calendar days, so dates alone cannot prove order; judge with the body. When unsure between SAME_OCCURRENCE and SAME_STORY, ask: if both reports are true, did one thing happen in the world, or two directly related things one after the other? A product's life cycle is not one event by itself.
